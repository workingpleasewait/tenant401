What happens after you file

DHCR moves slowly. This is normal. Here is what to watch for in the mail.

Keep paying your full lawful rent until DHCR mails you a written rent-reduction order. You may not reduce your rent on your own before that order arrives. After the order, pay the reduced amount stated in it.
A real timeline from this building

This is what happened in Docket MP 210039 B. Your case may move faster or slower.

In this case: within a few days
Docketing notice arrives by mail
DHCR confirms your complaint was received and assigns it a docket number.
→ Save this letter. Write down the docket number.
In this case: about 3 months after filing
DHCR serves the complaint on the owner
DHCR formally delivers your complaint to the landlord and gives them time to respond.
→ No action needed from you yet. Watch the mail.
Important — happens quietly
Your effective date is locked
For rent-stabilized tenants, your rent reduction is retroactive to the first of the month after DHCR serves the owner — months before you receive the order. In this case, service happened July 16; the effective date was August 1, 2024.

Rent-controlled tenants: your effective date is the first of the month after the order is issued, not the service date. See DHCR Fact Sheet #14 for details.
→ No action needed. This date is set by DHCR.
Possible — may or may not happen
DHCR may send you the owner's answer
If the landlord's response is relevant, DHCR may forward it to you and give you a deadline to reply.
→ Open every DHCR letter immediately and respond by the deadline stated.
In this case: about 5 months after filing
DHCR inspector visits the building
An inspector documents the elevator condition. In this case the inspection found the elevator bouncing and stopping several inches below floor level — a trip hazard.
→ The inspector does not need access to your apartment for an elevator complaint.
In this case: about 6 months after filing
DHCR mails you the rent reduction order
The order names your docket number, the effective date, the conditions found, and directs the owner to restore service. In this case: filed April 16 → order issued October 11, 2024.
→ Save this order. It is your legal document. You now pay the reduced rent stated in it.
After the order — ongoing
The order stays in effect until DHCR issues a restoration order
The landlord must apply to DHCR to have the rent restored. For rent-stabilized tenants, DHCR will not issue a restoration order until all services listed in the reduction order are corrected. If the landlord does not restore service within 30 days of the order, you can file Form RA-22.1 to request a compliance proceeding.
→ Continue paying the reduced rent. Watch for any DHCR letters about a restoration application.
Open every DHCR letter by its stated deadline. DHCR may ask you to respond to the owner's answer or confirm your address. Missing a deadline can affect your case.
📋 Keep a file with all of these
  • Docketing notice (with your case number)
  • Any letters from DHCR during the process
  • The rent reduction order when it arrives
  • Your 311 Service Request numbers
  • Certified mail receipts and return cards
