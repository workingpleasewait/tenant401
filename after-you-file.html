What happens after you file

Watch your mail and keep every DHCR letter.

Already have a rent-reduction order? Jump to what happens next →

Keep paying your full lawful rent until DHCR mails a written order. Then follow the rent and effective date in the order.
A real timeline from this building

This is what happened in Docket MP 210039 B. Your case may move faster or slower.

  1. In this case: within a few days
    Docketing notice arrives by mail
    DHCR assigns your docket number.
    → Save the notice and docket number.
  2. In this case: about 3 months after filing
    DHCR serves the complaint on the owner
    DHCR formally delivers your complaint to the landlord. This date matters: for rent-stabilized tenants, the rent reduction is retroactive to the first of the month after this service date — months before any order arrives. In this case, service happened July 16; the effective date was August 1, 2024.

    Rent-controlled tenants: your effective date is the first of the month after the order is issued, not the service date.

    DHCR may also send you the owner's answer and ask for a reply.
    → Watch the mail. If DHCR asks for a reply, meet the stated deadline.
  3. In this case: about 5 months after filing
    DHCR inspector visits the building
    DHCR may inspect the elevator.
    → No apartment access is needed for an elevator complaint.
  4. In this case: about 6 months after filing
    DHCR mails you the rent reduction order
    The order names the docket number, effective date, conditions found, and directs the owner to restore service. In this case: filed April 16 → order issued October 11, 2024.
    → Save the order and pay the rent stated in it.
After the order: Keep paying the reduced rent until DHCR issues a restoration order.
Owner wants to restore the rent?

The owner must apply to DHCR. DHCR will contact you. If the elevator still has a problem covered by the rent-reduction order:

  • State that elevator service has not been restored
  • Describe exactly what is still happening
  • Attach recent 311 numbers and any other records
  • Return your response by DHCR's stated deadline

This is part of your existing case. You do not need to start a new one. Do not sign or agree to anything stating service was restored unless that is true.

Keep paying the reduced rent unless DHCR issues a written restoration order. Your response tells DHCR that you dispute the owner's claim; DHCR makes the final decision.
